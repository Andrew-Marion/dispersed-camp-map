Dispersed Camp Map

Public land layer

Color-coded by who manages the land. Use the layers button on the map for the legend, to hide land types, and to add wildfire perimeters. Tap the map to see who manages a spot.

Dispersed camping is a general rule, not a permit. Check the local ranger district or field office for fire bans, seasonal closures, and posted limits. Private land is never shown as open.

Map

Detail loads from the internet and zooms in to street and trail level. Areas you view with Topo, Streets, or USGS are kept on this device and show again offline.

Trails and hikes

Zoom in to see trail maps: marked hiking routes and Forest Service trails. Tap Update this view to load named hiking routes as map points, then tap one for details and to draw its route. Tap the map to see the trail name and length under your finger.

AllTrails data is not used. It has no public data feed and its terms block copying. Each hike has a search link so you can check AllTrails yourself.

Offline regions

Save the map you are looking at so it works with no signal. Zoom in on a forest or county first. OpenStreetMap and OpenTopoMap do not allow bulk saving, so regions use USGS topo (US only) plus the land layers.

Tracks

Record your route in and out of a site with GPS. Keep the screen on and this page open, because phones pause location when the screen locks.

Cell coverage

No free coverage map is available to this page. Turn on the Cell towers chip to see towers mapped in OpenStreetMap, which is incomplete. Rate signal on your own pins, and check your carrier's map before you go.

Update camping data

Load free and basic campsites, named hiking routes, water points, and cell towers from OpenStreetMap for the part of the map on screen. They are saved here and work offline afterward.

My pins

Import GPX, GeoJSON, or CSV (name, lat, lon, notes) from other apps. Imported points become your pins.

About the data

Land ownership: BLM Surface Management Agency map (BLM, Forest Service, Park Service, Fish and Wildlife, Reclamation, BIA, military, state, local). Fires: National Interagency Fire Center. Forest roads: Forest Service motor vehicle use maps. US layers cover the United States only. Canada: Ontario Crown land (Ontario LIO), BC Crown land ownership and recreation sites (BC government), and parks and protected areas (Environment and Climate Change Canada). Other provinces are not included yet. Hikes, water points, and cell towers: OpenStreetMap. Hiking route tiles: Waymarked Trails. Forest Service trails: US Forest Service.

The built-in list holds 120 regional anchors, not exact campsites. Rules and closures change often.

Base maps: OpenTopoMap, OpenStreetMap contributors, USGS The National Map, Esri imagery. Natural Earth for the offline fallback.
